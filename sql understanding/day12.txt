First, Let's Recall GROUP BY
Suppose you write:
SELECT department, COUNT(*)
FROM employees
GROUP BY department;
Output:
department	COUNT(*)
Finance	1
HR	2
IT	2
Sales	1
Now imagine your manager says:
"Show only the departments that have at least 2 employees."
Can WHERE do that?
Wrong Way ❌
Many beginners try:
SELECT department, COUNT(*)
FROM employees
WHERE COUNT(*) >= 2
GROUP BY department;
SQLite returns an error similar to:
misuse of aggregate: COUNT()
Why?
Remember the SQL execution order.
SQL roughly processes queries like this:
FROM
WHERE
GROUP BY
Aggregate functions (COUNT, SUM, AVG, ...)
HAVING
SELECT
ORDER BY
Notice:
👉 WHERE runs before COUNT() is calculated.
At the time WHERE executes, SQL does not know the result of COUNT(*).
That's Why HAVING Exists
HAVING filters groups, not individual rows.
Correct query:
SELECT department,
       COUNT(*) AS Total_Employees
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 2;
Output:
department	Total_Employees
HR	2
IT	2
Think of It Like This
Imagine a classroom.
Students:
IT:
Rahul
Neha

HR:
Amit
Rohit

Sales:
Priya

Finance:
Vikas
GROUP BY creates the groups.
Then HAVING says:
"Keep only groups that satisfy my condition."
Since only IT and HR have 2 employees, Sales and Finance are removed.
WHERE vs HAVING
This is one of the most asked interview questions.
WHERE	HAVING
Filters rows	Filters groups
Runs before GROUP BY	Runs after GROUP BY
Cannot use aggregate functions like COUNT()	Can use aggregate functions
Used on individual records	Used on grouped results
Example 1 – WHERE
Find employees earning more than ₹50,000.
SELECT *
FROM employees
WHERE salary > 50000;
Output:
name	salary
Neha	70000
Rohit	55000
Vikas	90000
WHERE removes rows before grouping.
Example 2 – HAVING
Find departments whose average salary is greater than ₹55,000.
SELECT department,
       AVG(salary) AS Average_Salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
HAVING AVG(salary) > 55000;
Output:
department	Average_Salary
Finance	90000
IT	60000
Example 3 – WHERE + HAVING Together
Find departments where:
employees earn at least ₹45,000
average salary is greater than ₹55,000
SELECT department,
       AVG(salary) AS Average_Salary
FROM employees
WHERE salary >= 45000
GROUP BY department
HAVING AVG(salary) > 55000;
Step 1 (WHERE)
Remove Priya (₹40,000).
Remaining:
name	department	salary

Amit	HR	45000
Neha	IT	70000
Vikas	Finance	90000
Rohit	HR	55000
Step 2 (GROUP BY)
Groups:

Finance
Step 3 (AVG)
IT → 60,000
HR → 50,000

Step 4 (HAVING)
Keep only averages above ₹55,000.

HAVING with COUNT()
Departments having at least 2 employees.
SELECT department,
       COUNT(*) AS Total
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 2;

Mini Challenge (with Answers)
Q1. Find departments with more than one employee and an average salary greater than ₹55,000.
SELECT department,
       COUNT(*) AS Total_Employees,
       AVG(salary) AS Average_Salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1
   AND AVG(salary) > 55000;
